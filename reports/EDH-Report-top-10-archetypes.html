🎴 Top 10 Commander Archetypes — EDH Report

This report breaks down the 10 best Commander archetypes from the Yoonns MTG ranking, in English. For each archetype you get the deck strategy (principle, core strengths, key weakness) plus the 5 most popular generals by EDHREC deck count. Sources: EDHREC (deck counts), Scryfall (Oracle text & card art), and the Yoonns MTG video "Les 10 meilleurs types de decks COMMANDER !" (translated into English).

🪙 Tokens — Video #1
  • Strategy core: Multiply small creatures across the board, then amplify their stats in one explosive turn or convert the swarm into direct damage.
  • Main strengths: Ease and redundancy — a wall of blockers, draw engines like Skullclamp, and several game-enders (Craterhoof-style pumps, mass evasion, mass sacrifice) make the deck hard to stop.
  • Key weakness: A single board wipe resets the field — but the deck rebuilds fast because the command zone keeps feeding the engine.
  • Top general: Lathril, Blade of the Elves is the archetype's biggest draw, pairing live elf-token production with built-in drain.

☠️ Poison — Video #2
  • Strategy core: Win by landing 10 poison counters instead of draining 40 life, effectively quartering the victory threshold.
  • Main strengths: With proliferate, a single counter can finish off every opponent from afar without needing to attack again.
  • Key weakness: Immediate political liability — the whole table gangs up on you as soon as the first counters start landing.
  • Top general: Fynn, the Fangbearer turbo-charges the plan by turning every deathtouch hit into two poison counters.

🧟 Reanimator — Video #3
  • Strategy core: Dump big creatures into the graveyard, then bring them back for a fraction of their mana cost.
  • Main strengths: Blistering speed — an Archon of Cruelty can hit the board by turn four — plus easy reanimation loops and infinite combos.
  • Key weakness: Sensitive to graveyard hate, which casual pods don't always run in quantity; one well-timed exile can stall the plan.
  • Top general: Meren of Clan Nel Toth grinds constant value by returning creatures on end step, ramping into the reanimate plan.

⛰️ Ramp/Landfall — Video #4

  • Key weakness: Can stall before the curve if the initial ramp spells don't show up in the opening hand.

🌿 Enchantress — Video #5

🌀 Blink — Video #7
  • Strategy core: Temporarily exile your creatures so they return and re-trigger their enter-the-battlefield (ETB) effects.
  • Main strengths: A phenomenal card-advantage and control engine, and one of the hardest decks to interact with — blinking in response thwarts removal.
  • Key weakness: Often lacks fast, direct win conditions, especially in Azorius (White-Blue).

💀 Aristocrats — Video #9
  • Strategy core: Continuously sacrifice your own creatures to trigger recurring effects — life loss, mana, and card draw.
  • Main strengths: Access to powerful pieces that dodge the ban hammer (Zulaport Cutthroat, Phyrexian Altar, Dictate of Erebos, Blood Artist), skip combat by draining at range, and thrive on the graveyard.
  • Key weakness: Needs several key pieces — sacrifice fodder, an engine, and a wincon — so a bad opening hand can derail the game.
  • Top general: Chatterfang, Squirrel General generates a near-infinite stream of sacrifice fodder all on its own.
